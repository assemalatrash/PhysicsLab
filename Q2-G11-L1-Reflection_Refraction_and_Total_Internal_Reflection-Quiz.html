💡 Lesson Hints & Formulas

1. The Normal Line & Law of Reflection

  • The Normal Line is perpendicular (90°) to the mirror or boundary surface.
  • Golden Rule: Always measure angles from the Normal Line—never from the surface! If an angle is given from the surface, subtract it from 90°.
  • Law of Reflection: θi = θr

2. Index of Refraction (n)

  • Measures how much a transparent material slows down light compared to vacuum (c = 300,000 km/s).
  • n = cv   and   v = cn
  • n is unitless and always ≥ 1 (nair = 1). Higher n means slower light!

3. Refraction & Snell's Law

  • Fast → Slow (n1 < n2, e.g., Air to Water/Glass): Light slows down and bends TOWARD the normal (θ2 < θ1).
  • Slow → Fast (n1 > n2, e.g., Water/Glass to Air): Light speeds up and bends AWAY from the normal (θ2 > θ1).
  • Snell's Law: n1 · sin θ1 = n2 · sin θ2
  • Rearranged forms: n2 = n1 · sin θ1sin θ2   and   sin θ2 = n1 · sin θ1n2

4. Total Internal Reflection (TIR) & Critical Angle (θc)

  • TIR can ONLY happen when light goes from a higher index (n1) to a lower index (n2), so n1 > n2 (e.g., fiber-optic core to cladding, or glass to air).
  • At the exact critical angle (θc), the refracted ray skims the surface at θ2 = 90°.
  • Critical Angle Formula: sin θc = n2n1
  • If θ1 > θc, 100% of the light reflects back inside!
