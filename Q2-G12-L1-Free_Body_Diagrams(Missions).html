Not Solved

Hint: By Newton's First Law (inertia), an object moving at constant velocity on frictionless ice needs zero horizontal force to keep moving! Only the two balanced vertical forces belong on the FBD.

Not Solved

Hint: Nobody is pushing the crate anymore! Kinetic friction always opposes the direction of sliding (so it points left). Vertically, W and N remain balanced.

3A Apparent Weight Concept

5 pts

A 50 kg passenger (W = 500 N) rides upward inside an elevator at a steady constant velocity of 4 (m/s). How does the upward Normal Force (N) arrow compare to W?

Hint: Constant velocity means a = 0 (m/s²), so there is no net force. Upward arrow equals downward arrow!

4A Ramp FBD Rules

5 pts

On an official AP Free Body Diagram of a block resting on a tilted ramp, which statement is 100% accurate?

Hint: Gravity always pulls straight toward Earth's center, Normal force is always 90° to the surface, and N = W · cos θ is always less than W on a tilt!

4B 30° Smooth Ramp (W∥)

5 pts

A shipping crate of weight W = 120 (N) sits on a smooth 30° ramp (sin 30° = 1/2). Calculate the parallel weight component W∥ pulling it downhill.

Line 1: W∥ = W · sin θ
Line 2: W∥ = 120 · 1/2
Line 3: W∥ = (N)
Hint: Multiply 120 by 1/2 (half of 120)!

4C Magic 3-4-5 Ramp (N)

5 pts

A 50 N block rests on a ramp shaped like a 3-4-5 right triangle (cos θ = 4/5). Calculate the Normal Force N exerted by the ramp.

Line 1: N = W · cos θ
Line 2: N = 50 · 4/5
Line 3: N = (N)
Hint: Divide 50 by 5 first (= 10), then multiply by 4!

4D 200 N Crate on 3-4-5 Ramp

5 pts

A 200 N crate rests on a 3-4-5 ramp (sin θ = 3/5, cos θ = 4/5). Calculate BOTH W∥ and N.

W∥ = 200 · 3/5 = (N)
N = 200 · 4/5 = (N)
Hint: 200 / 5 = 40. So W∥ = 40 · 3 and N = 40 · 4!
